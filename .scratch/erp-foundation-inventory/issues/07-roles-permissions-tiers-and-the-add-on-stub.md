# 07 — Roles, permissions, tiers, and the add-on shape stub

**What to build:** Control over what my colleagues can do, and which modules a company sees at all.

I can invite people into my company, create roles by hand, choose which permissions each role
holds, and assign roles to people. A stock clerk can record movements but cannot change cost
prices, and need not see HRM at all. Permissions come from the modules that declare them in their
manifests, so a new module extends the permission list without anything central being edited —
the only way this survives forty modules.

Alongside it, tier-based enablement: a company sits in a tier and reaches the modules that tier
grants. Navigation shows only what is available, endpoints of unavailable modules are refused, and
the application behaves correctly when a module simply is not there for that company.

The **add-on shape stub** is built here as the test case — Custom tier, optional, depending on
another module and extending it through its public contract without editing it. It must be
unavailable to a Core-tier company while everything else keeps working. That is the second of the
two shapes the platform must never harden away from.

**Blocked by:** 06 — Products and units of measure (Core), and the module generator

**Status:** ready-for-agent

- [ ] I can invite a colleague into my company and they can sign in
- [ ] I can create a role, choose the permissions it holds, edit it, and assign it to people
- [ ] The permission list is assembled from module manifests, with no central list to edit
- [ ] Permissions are checked at the API boundary in one consistent way on every endpoint
- [ ] The conformance pack proves a module's endpoints all check permissions
- [ ] An unauthorized action is refused with a clear reason, not a generic failure
- [ ] The UI hides or disables actions the signed-in user lacks permission for
- [ ] A role can be denied a whole module, not just individual actions
- [ ] The HRM stub's sensitive field is visible only to roles granted it
- [ ] The company creator always retains full access and cannot be locked out
- [ ] A role in use cannot be deleted without reassigning the people holding it
- [ ] A company has a tier, and the tier determines which modules are available to it
- [ ] Navigation shows only modules the tier allows and the user may access
- [ ] Endpoints of an unavailable module are refused with a clear reason
- [ ] A module whose dependency is unavailable to that company is itself unavailable
- [ ] Changing a company's tier changes what it can reach, without a restart
- [ ] No module needs to know tiers exist; availability is enforced by the foundation
- [ ] The add-on shape stub exists at Custom tier, generated by the generator, depending on and
      extending another module through its public contract without editing it
- [ ] The add-on stub is unavailable at Core tier and available at Custom tier
- [ ] Backend tests cover per-endpoint refusal, module-level denial, restricted fields, tier-based
      refusal and dependency-driven unavailability
- [ ] Frontend tests cover role management, invitations, and navigation differing by tier and role
